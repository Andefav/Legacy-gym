First Time in a Gym? 4 Golden Equipment Safety Rules

Review these quick tips before picking up a weight or stepping on a machine.

Rule 01

Spot the Yellow Adjustment Pins

Every machine has bright yellow or red knobs. Pull them to adjust the seat height so the machine's pivot bolt lines up with your knee or elbow joints. Listen for the "click" into place.

Rule 02

The Magnetic Stack Pin

On selectorized cable machines, always ensure the weight pin is inserted 100% horizontally and flush into the plate. Never leave it dangling halfway out.

Rule 03

Control the 3-Second Return

Never let the weight plates slam together at the bottom of a repetition. Lower the weight smoothly over 3 seconds; this protects your joints and builds stronger muscles faster.

Rule 04

Cardio Emergency Safety Clip

Before hitting "Quick Start" on any treadmill, attach the red safety clip to your shirt or waistline. If you stumble or slip, the safety cord automatically halts the belt immediately.

Beginner Equipment Safety Tip: Picking Up & Racking Dumbbells

When taking dumbbells off the rack, always stand directly in front of the weight, bend at your knees (not your lower back), and grasp the handles securely before stepping back. When finished, walk the weights back to the rack under full control — never drop dumbbells onto the rubber floor.

Beginner Equipment Safety Tip: Safe Treadmill Boarding & Dismounting

Never step directly onto a moving treadmill belt. When starting, stand with feet on the wide plastic side rails, clip the emergency safety lanyard to your clothes, select your speed, and step on only once the belt begins rolling smoothly. Use the big red "STOP" button to finish.

Beginner Equipment Safety Tip: The Joint Alignment & Pin Rule

Every single machine at Legacy Forge has a visual diagram placard on the frame. Check the diagram before sitting down. Always verify that your joints match the red/yellow rotational indicator on the machine axis, and select a starting weight that allows 12 smooth, non-straining repetitions.
